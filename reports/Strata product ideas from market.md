# Strata should prove answers against real reports

*Research report, 20 November 2026. Built from the four notes in `research_notes/Strata product ideas from market/` (ontology, semantic, context, accuracy testing). It also reuses still-valid points from `reports/Enterprise Context Factory roadmap.md`. Where the two disagree, the new notes win.*

## Summary in simple English

The market in 2025-2026 has one clear pattern. The AI writes a first draft. A small set of definitions is made exact and certified. Every answer is tested against "golden" questions. Every vendor (Databricks, Snowflake, Microsoft, Google, ThoughtSpot, Omni, Lightdash, Atlan) now has some version of this loop.

Strata already has most of the parts. It checks every AI claim against the data. It certifies metrics with an owner. It rejects numbers that have no source. No vendor we found shows a per-claim "% confirmed by the data" score. That is Strata's strongest point.

The biggest open gap in the market is this: **every vendor's test tool assumes the tester can write or read SQL.** Business people do not know SQL. They know the numbers in their monthly report. No product we found lets a user upload a report and have the system check its own answers against every number in it. Strata can own this.

So the manual report-check form should become **"Prove it against my report"**:

1. The user drops in an Excel or CSV report they already trust.
2. Strata finds each number, its label, period, unit and rounding.
3. Strata writes the question for each number.
4. The user ticks the numbers to trust on one screen. No typing.
5. Strata runs every question three times and gives a plain verdict: Match, Off by a unit, Different definition, Wrong number, Refused, or Unstable.
6. For each failure, the user picks who is right. Strata proposes the smallest fix and re-tests it at once.
7. The ticked numbers become a guard. They re-run when the ontology, metrics or data change.
8. Everyday answers then show a badge such as "Matches Q3 pack, checked 2 days ago".

**Top 5 to build next:**

1. "Prove it against my report" (replaces the form). Effort M for Excel/CSV.
2. One test suite for everything, with history and a "was right, now wrong" counter. Effort M.
3. A trust badge on every answer and a source behind every number. Effort S.
4. "Fix it" feedback that becomes a proposed change for an owner to approve. Effort M.
5. A scheduled drift re-check for the ontology with a "what changed" view. Effort M.

All five follow Strata's rule: **AI proposes, data checks, person decides.** None needs Docker, a server or a new main screen.

**A warning about the sources.** Almost all evidence comes from vendors describing their own products. Accuracy numbers (for example "0% to 100%") come from the vendor that sells the feature. Strata should not quote them. It should make its own numbers on real client reports.

*Effort scale used below (small Python/React team): **S** = a few days to one week, **M** = one to three weeks, **L** = one to two months. "Extend" means Strata already has a part of it; "New" means nothing exists yet.*

## The market converged on draft, certify, test, and Strata already owns the "test against data" step

The 2025-2026 products look different on the surface but share the same loop. Microsoft Fabric IQ has a "Generate Ontology" button on a Power BI model, and then lists what the user must still do by hand: review keys, bind relationships, rename to friendly names ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/iq/ontology/concepts-generate)). Its ontology agent has a **"Test the ontology"** step that writes sample questions and runs them against the bound data. Databricks Genie Ontology splits **"modeled knowledge"**, the small set of definitions that must be exact, from **"learned knowledge"** mined from dashboards and SQL, and ranks conflicting definitions with OntoRank ([Databricks](https://databricks.com/blog/announcing-ga-genie-ontology)). Snowflake mines query history to propose metrics, and GA'd this as Semantic View Autopilot on 3 February 2026 ([Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/semantic-view-autopilot/)). Tableau's Pulse agent never lets the LLM compute; a deterministic service computes and the LLM only explains ([Tableau](https://www.tableau.com/es-es/blog/tableau-pulse-enhanced-qa)).

Three things stand out for Strata. First, **nobody shows a per-claim "data confirms this" percentage**. Fabric checks nulls, ranges and uniqueness on bindings ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/iq/ontology/how-to-bind-data)), and Atlan shows Verified/Draft/Deprecated certificates ([Atlan](https://docs.atlan.com/product/capabilities/discovery/how-tos/add-certificates)), but neither tells you how much of a claim the data supports. Second, **verified question-to-answer pairs are in every product** (Snowflake verified queries, Genie trusted assets, Fabric example queries, Looker golden queries, Wren SQL pairs), and Fabric's example queries do **not work on ontology sources** ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/fabric/data-science/data-agent-example-queries)). Strata's verified questions already live over an ontology, which is a real point of difference. Third, **feedback is becoming a proposed change, not silent learning**. Omni turns thumbs-down into a P1/P2 list of model edits linked to the chats that caused them ([Omni](https://docs.omni.co/ai/suggestions.md)); Genie says plainly that it "does not automatically learn or change its behavior in response to feedback" ([Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/genie-agents/talk-to-genie)). This matches Strata's "person decides" rule.

## The knowledge graph is the evidence layer behind all three

Strata's graph is virtual over live tables, so it costs nothing extra to keep in sync. Its job in the market pattern is to supply **evidence and paths**, not to be a separate product. Four uses stand out. First, **graph paths are Recipes**: when an answer is right, the path it followed (Customer → Sold-To → Order → Item) is exactly what ThoughtSpot stores as a Recipe, so saving a verified answer should save its path too (Context idea 3). Second, the graph gives **merge evidence**: shared key values between two tables are the proof behind an entity-resolution suggestion (Ontology idea 10), the same job Neo4j's resolver does ([neo4j-graphrag](https://neo4j.com/docs/neo4j-graphrag-python/current/user_guide_kg_builder.html)). Third, a one-page **record view** per business thing (fields, linked things, claim status, owner, recent changes) is Palantir's Object View at laptop scale ([Palantir](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/ontology/overview/)), and it is where a user lands when they click "show source" on a number. Fourth, the same node-and-link model can carry **"used by" lineage** (concept → column → metric → pack → agent → answer), which is what makes deprecation flags and impact previews possible. Keep this inside the existing graph screen, per the simple-screens rule. Strata already has drill-down, paging, search and path-between-two-records, so all four are extensions. A graph database is still not needed.

## Replace the report-check form with "Prove it against my report"

### Why the form falls short, and where the market is weak

Every vendor test tool asks for SQL or code. Databricks benchmarks take a question plus SQL answer, compare result sets at 4 significant digits, and cap at 500 questions per space ([Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/genie/benchmarks)). Snowflake judges executed SQL with an LLM and shows accuracy, regressions and latency per run ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-analyst-evaluations)). Fabric's test is a Python SDK with a `question, expected_answer` CSV and needs F2+ capacity ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/data-science/evaluate-data-agent)). Fabric is closest to a business user, because its expected answer is a number, not SQL, but the user still has to build the CSV by hand. Atlan says it generates "100s of questions" from dashboards and SQL history, but this is marketing with no public screens or numbers ([Atlan](https://atlan.com/context-engineering-studio/)). **We found no product that reads an Excel or PDF report and checks its own answers against every number in it.** That is absence of evidence from about 18 searches, not proof, but the gap looks real.

Strata's current form already scores at the report's own precision and detects unit errors, which no vendor documents. Its weakness is that the user must type each question and each number. The owner's "make it better" points at exactly that. The fix is to remove the typing and turn the result into decisions, not a score.

### The recommended flow, step by step

| Step | What the user sees | What Strata does (automatic) | What the person decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Start | One button: **"Prove it against my report"**. Two doors: "Drop a report" or "No report? Let Strata suggest checks." | — | Which door |
| 2. Read the report | A progress line: "Found 46 numbers in 3 sheets." | Reads xlsx/csv (PDF later). For each number captures label, row/column headers, period, filters, unit, displayed rounding: e.g. (Revenue, EMEA, Q3 2026, EUR thousands, 1 dp) = 4,812.3 | — |
| 3. Strata reads it back | Each number as a sentence: **"Strata reads this as: Net revenue, region = EMEA, Jul-Sep 2026, in EUR thousands."** Linked metric and ontology things shown as chips. | Writes the question, maps it to certified metrics and ontology terms, pins relative dates ("last quarter") to absolute ones, ranks totals and KPIs first | — |
| 4. Tick the truth | One screen, report thumbnail on the left with the number highlighted, a checklist on the right: "Use these 12 numbers as the truth?" Default: top 12 ticked. | — | Untick stale or wrong numbers; fix a mis-read label with one dropdown. **About one minute; no typing.** |
| 5. Run | "Checking 12 numbers..." with a live count | Runs each question fresh, three times, plus two paraphrases. Keeps these questions out of the AI's examples so the score is honest. Strata's own counting query runs first as a control | — |
| 6. Scorecard | Headline: **"11 of 12 numbers match your report."** Wrong numbers on top in red. | Verdict per line: **Match** (within report precision) · **Off by a unit** (x1000, %, currency) · **Different definition** (another governed metric matches, e.g. gross vs net) · **Wrong number** (silent error) · **Refused** (shown as a coverage gap, not a failure) · **Unstable** (runs or phrasings disagree) | — |
| 7. Who is right? | For each failure, three buttons: **"Report is right"** · **"Report is old or wrong"** · **"Both right, different definitions"** | Proposes the smallest fix for the first and third choice: synonym, default filter, unit, metric mapping, or a named metric variant ("Revenue (finance pack)") | Picks a side, approves the fix |
| 8. Re-test now | The line flips to Match (or not) in place | Re-runs that question and its paraphrases against the fix before saving | Keeps or rolls back |
| 9. Keep as a guard | "Saved as **Q3 pack** — 12 checks." Sparkline of history and a **"was right, now wrong"** counter | Re-runs when ontology, metrics, notes or data change, or nightly. `strata check --suite q3-pack` returns non-zero on regression for scripts and CI | Who gets told |
| 10. Trust in daily use | Chat answers covered by a passing check show **"Matches Q3 pack ✓ (checked 2 days ago)"**; others show "Not yet checked" with "Check against a report" | Links badge to the stored check, not to a model confidence score | — |

The "No report?" door uses what Strata already has: questions suggested from the data, certified metrics, competency questions from the ontology, and confirmed chat answers ("This number is right → keep checking it", the Databricks "Add as benchmark" pattern). Its ground truth is Strata's own deterministic counting query, labelled as such, so the user knows it checks consistency, not business truth.

### Design choices backed by the evidence

Four details matter more than they look. **A mismatch means "investigate", not "AI wrong"**: independent work found 52.8% to 66% annotation errors in public text-to-SQL benchmarks ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778)), so ground truth is often the broken part, and reports can be stale too. That is why step 7 asks who is right. **Run several times**: Snowflake advises learning a set's normal range before setting a pass threshold, because generation is non-deterministic. **Do not sell consistency as confidence**: a July 2026 study puts self-consistency signals at only about 0.61-0.68 AUROC as a predictor of correctness, while verification-style signals do better ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06799)); this figure came from a search snippet, not a full read. So "Unstable" is a flag, not a probability. **Hold the test out**: Snowflake removes evaluation queries from the model's context during a run, and Databricks keeps benchmark SQL out of Genie's context ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/how-build-production-ready-genie-spaces-and-build-trust-along-way)). A fix that pastes the answer into context would fake a pass.

One threshold is worth borrowing as a talking point only. Databricks recommends above 80% benchmark accuracy before user acceptance testing, and 10-20 questions per set (same source). It is vendor advice, but it gives clients a familiar bar.

## Users complain about silent errors, upkeep and cost, and each opens a door for a challenger

The complaints fall into five groups, and Strata has a specific answer to each. The evidence is thin on direct user voice: Reddit and Hacker News searches found almost nothing usable, and most complaint framing comes from vendors who sell the fix.

**Silent wrong numbers** come first (OWOX, Towards Data Science and Genie's own docs above). The opening for a challenger is to make every number traceable and to test against numbers the business already published. **Upkeep and drift** come second: Thoughtworks' "stale within two quarters" and Stardog's "the business changes, the data changes, and the definitions drift" ([Stardog](https://www.stardog.com/blog/25-years-in-semantics/)). Strata's checks against live data are an automatic re-validation trigger, which is the missing piece. **Definitions disagree across tools**, as the 3-of-4 MetricFlow/Cube/LookML/DataJunction result shows; dbt itself admits a poorly built semantic layer can make consistency worse ([dbt](https://www.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-pitfalls)). Strata's receipt plus an export cross-check answers this. **Cost, complexity and learning curve**: Palantir reviewers report a steep learning curve and opaque licensing, and one AWS Marketplace unit is priced at $100k/month ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-a5m5xespbqyci)); the dbt Semantic Layer needs a $100/user/month plan ([Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/semantic-layer/dbt-semantic-layer-vs-cube/)). A pip-installed, laptop-scale tool with no licence is a direct answer. **Hard product limits** frustrate users: Genie spaces cap at 50 tables and cannot mix metric views with other tables ([Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/genie/trusted-assets)), Fabric agents allow five data sources, and Fabric example queries do not work on ontology sources.

The steelman for the incumbents is real. They sit where the data already lives, they have security and row-level access built in, and their evaluation tools work at scale. Strata should not fight them on platform breadth. It wins where they are weakest: **testing by business users, data-checked claims, and governed learning that a person approves.** It can also sit on top of them, since its exports (MetricFlow, Ossie, MCP packs) feed their agents.

## Vendor bias and what we do not know

Almost every source is a vendor describing its own product: Palantir, Microsoft, Databricks, Snowflake, Google, ThoughtSpot, Omni, Lightdash, Stardog, Timbr, Wren, Hex, Atlan and OWOX. Atlan also writes about competitors (Genie, Timbr, dbt vs Cube), so it is biased twice. Vendor accuracy claims, such as Databricks' 0% → 54% → 77% → 100% on 13 questions, or Google's "up to two thirds fewer errors", should not be repeated to clients. Databricks' Genie Ontology GA status is itself disputed between sources, and its Ontology Manager is announced but not shipped. Atlan's Context Engineering Studio has no public screens or numbers. The independent evidence is small: G2, PeerSpot and Gartner reviews for Palantir and Kyvos, one Thoughtworks article, one HackerNoon test, one academic adoption study, and several arXiv papers on benchmarks and confidence. We found no independent before/after accuracy numbers for any vendor's teaching loop, no study of how users react to trust badges, and no product that tests against uploaded reports. Products not covered include Hex's testing UX, Zenlytic, Dot, Select Star, Secoda, Collibra, TopBraid and WebProtégé.

## Conclusion

The market has made "test the AI against golden answers" a standard feature, but it has built that feature for analysts who write SQL. The people who decide whether to trust Strata are not analysts. They are finance and sales managers who already hold the right answers in their monthly packs. That shifts Strata's best move from adding more AI features to making its existing strength, checking against data, visible and usable by those people. A report check that needs no typing, explains each miss in business words and becomes a permanent guard does more for trust than any confidence score.

It also gives Strata something no vendor source can: its own accuracy numbers, measured on the client's own reports. Those numbers are the honest replacement for the vendor claims this research found. They also feed back into every layer, because each "Different definition" verdict is a missing metric variant, each "Refused" is a gap in the ontology, and each "was right, now wrong" is drift caught before a user sees it.
